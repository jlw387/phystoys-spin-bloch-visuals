Prepare a geometric state

These controls prepare an eigenstate of the previous measurement axis. The new measurement uses the separate θ/φ controls. Preparing a state also updates the complex inputs. Rotated ±1 and 0 eigenstates entered through presets or complex inputs automatically orient the geometric shape and these controls. General superpositions do not yet have a geometric shape.

±1: sphere of radius ½ centered at ±s/2, counting uniform curved area. 0: a disk centered at the origin perpendicular to s (uniform area), or its circular rim (uniform length).

Measurement boundaries

Enter a finite real value of at least 0.05, or an expression such as sqrt(2)/2. Values above 4 expand the slider range. The geometry uses the full evaluated precision; very steep boundaries can exceed the mesh or sampling resolution.

u = r·n. The selected formula gives height h in the transverse coordinates x′, y′. Blue (+1): u > h. Red (−1): u < −h. Green (0): between them. Boundary ties count as 0. Surfaces are clipped to the unit ball.

The cross follows laboratory x/y rotated by the shortest rotation from z to the measurement axis (a fixed convention at −z). It never follows the state. For a z measurement its valleys lie on x and y; try 0(x), then prepare 0 with previous axis θ=90°, φ=45° to see the off-angle mismatch. The smooth-cross height is defined as zero at the origin.

Husimi surface / sphere of directions

This is the original exact quantum probability field, not the experimental geometry. Orange dots = Majorana stars; white arrow = ⟨S⟩. Drag either view to orbit independently.

The outcome buttons work in every view. With the amplitude surface enabled, radius squared equals the selected outcome's probability, with no minimum radius or maximum-based rescaling. P(+1|n) is the Husimi Q surface: try m=+1 (z) for the revolved cardioid, m=0 (z) for the pinched torus, or Intermediate stars. Selecting P(0|n) or P(−1|n) shows that outcome's amplitude surface instead. Turn amplitude off to use the original sphere/inflate controls. This is a radial probability readout, not an area-fraction model. Orange stars remain on the unit sphere of directions, not on the deformed surface.
