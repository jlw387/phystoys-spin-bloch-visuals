Unordered pairs on a circle: torus → Möbius strip

1. Two points on a circle

Drag either point. The chord joining them is the same chord whichever point you call 1 or 2.

Colored arcs: positive and negative sides of the dashed cut. Both points positive → ++; both negative → −−; one of each → mixed (gray).

2. Ordered pairs: torus

Going around the hole moves point 1; going around the tube moves point 2. The white curve is the diagonal, where both points coincide.

A pair and its swap are two separate markers, mirror images across the diagonal. Press Swap: the markers trade places. There are two gray regions: +− and −+.

3. Unordered pairs: Möbius strip

Glue each torus point to its mirror image across the diagonal. What remains is a Möbius strip. Press Swap: the white marker does not move.

The white curve is the strip's single edge: coincident pairs, the former diagonal. The two torus gray regions become one mixed region, leaving three regions for three outcomes.

How the strip coordinates work

For points at angles α and β, the chord joining them has normal direction (α + β)/2 and signed distance cos((β − α)/2) from the center. Neither changes when α and β are swapped, so these two numbers label the unordered pair. Going once around the circle reverses the chord's signed distance: that reversal is the strip's half-twist.

Spin-1 analogue: replace the circle by a sphere. Unordered pairs of points on a sphere form a smooth, closed four-dimensional space with no edge (ℂP²), which is the space of pure spin-1 states. The circle version is the lower-dimensional picture of the same gluing.
