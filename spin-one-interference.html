Spin-1 zero state: where interference enters

One fixed state, three measurement directions

The state is (|z+ z−⟩ + |z− z+⟩)/√2, or spin-1 |0⟩ along z. Its Majorana stars stay at +z and −z in every example.

Gold = first ordering (ab); purple = exchanged ordering (ba); white = their summed amplitude. Contributions below already include the 1/√2 normalization.

The faint wire spheres show the proposed mirrored anti-states: each lies exactly on the other star's state sphere. This overlap is identical in all three views. It is not used to calculate interference.

Red patches encode +|+n⟩, magenta +|−n⟩, and purple −|−n⟩ in the chosen phase convention. Gold and pale-purple arrows still label the two orderings. What the comparison shows

Geometric overlap stays fixed, but changing the measurement frame changes the signs and lengths of the amplitude contributions. Add those contributions first, then square the result.

The projection diagrams summarize this particular state: the longitudinal component cos θ gives P(0) = cos² θ; the transverse component sin θ gives P(+1) = P(−1) = sin² θ/2. They illustrate exact amplitude relations, not surface-area measurement regions.

The ++ and −− rows each correspond to one spin-1 outcome. The two mixed rows are orthogonal labeled-qubit outcomes: their probabilities add to P(0), not their amplitudes. Each row separately adds the ab and ba amplitudes coherently. The individual stars are state ingredients, not two independently measurable particles with fixed star marginals.
