Spatial sphere overlap (exploratory)

Moving this slider prepares one star at +z and the other tilted toward +x. Presets update the three complex inputs; all views follow the same state.

  • Lens: the solid region inside both spheres (mint).
  • Lens volume: the volume of that solid region, divided by one sphere's volume.
  • Lens surface area: the area of the lens's curved boundary (both mint caps), divided by one sphere's surface area.
  • Rim disk: the flat disk spanned by the circle where the two sphere surfaces cross (white). Its area divided by a sphere's central cross-section equals the quantum overlap |⟨a|b⟩|² exactly. The rim circle always passes through the origin, and its diameter (dashed) runs from the origin to the midpoint between the star tips.

Gold/purple spheres have radius ½ and centers at half their Majorana directions. Mint highlights the boundary of their shared interior (the lens), including the spherical patches inside the other ball. Opposite stars give spheres touching only at the origin; coincident stars give complete overlap. Colours identify the stars, not measurement outcomes.

The intersection volume, shared surface patches and quantum overlap are different quantities, displayed separately. This spatial overlay is for exploration and is not used to calculate measurement probabilities. Changing the measurement axis does not change it.

Probabilities as distance products

Same control as in the sphere-overlap panel: one star at +z, the other tilted toward +x. The measurement axis is still set at the top of the page.

Drag to orbit; scroll to zoom. Faint gold/purple spheres and solid star radii provide context. Dashed gold/purple guides project the star tips onto A and B. The white circle, centred at m with radius H, lies in the plane of the axis and m and passes through A and B; coloured lines are the tangents from the poles. White dashes show m; orange dashes show its perpendicular component. Mint d = (a − b)/2 joins the purple sphere center to the gold sphere center; its signed axial projection dₙ runs from m's foot to A.

How the probabilities arise: draw the circle centred at m through the projected tips A and B (its radius is the gray length H). The measurement axis cuts this circle at A and B, so by the power-of-a-point theorem the square on the tangent from −n has area |−n A| × |−n B| — the +1 numerator; the tangent from +n gives −1. Each pole, its tangent point and m form a right triangle, so the square on the segment from m to the pole splits into a tangent square (±1) and a radius square H² (one mixed ordering). The two radius squares together give 0. The four leg squares add up to |m + n|² + |m − n|² = 2(1 + |m|²), whatever the axis; each probability is its share of that total.

The 2D drawing lies in the plane containing the measurement axis and m, so every length is drawn true to scale (unlike the perspective view). Dotted lines join m to the poles (the right triangles' hypotenuses). The orange dashed leg is |m⊥| and the mint bar is |dₙ|, so H² = |m⊥|² + dₙ². The full d generally lies outside this plane and is shown in 3D. P(0) vanishes exactly when the circle collapses to a point (A = B at m's foot); P(±1) vanishes when the opposite pole lies on the circle. At coincident stars, m is a limiting rim point; at opposite stars it is the origin. This is an exact rewriting of the Born-rule probabilities, not a region-cutting model: the circle's radius changes with the measurement axis, only the total stays fixed.

Combined amplitudes and probability areas

Uses the shared measurement axis above. Colours: z blue/cyan, y green/yellow, x red/magenta; custom directions blend these RGB colours using weights nₓ², nᵧ², n_z². Zero stays gray. Opposite axes have the same palette; their outcome probabilities exchange. Colours identify the axis, not quantum phases.

Each tile is one ordered pair outcome. Add the contributions from |ab⟩ and |ba⟩ as complex arrows, then square the resultant magnitude. Both contributions include the common normalization 1/√(2(1 + |⟨a|b⟩|²)).

Gold arrow: |ab⟩ contribution from the origin. Purple arrow: |ba⟩ contribution starting at the gold arrow's tip. White arrow: their sum from the origin. All tiles share one scale and use Re horizontally, Im vertically. The spinor phase convention fixes the arrows' appearance; probabilities do not depend on that convention.
